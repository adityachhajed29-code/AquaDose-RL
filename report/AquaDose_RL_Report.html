AquaDose-RL: Continuous Proximal Policy Optimization for Non-Linear pH Neutralization and Regulatory Compliance in Industrial Wastewater Treatment
Group 14: Aditya Chhajed (B24CM1073)  •  Vedansh Buchasia (B24CM1066)
Department of Artificial Intelligence & Data Science
GitHub Repository: https://github.com/adityachhajed29-code/AquaDose-RL
Abstract— Industrial wastewater neutralization in Continuous Stirred-Tank Reactors (CSTRs) presents an extreme non-linear control challenge due to logarithmic chemical titration curves, asymmetric buffer equilibria, and severe stochastic influent flow and pH shock loads. Classical PID controllers frequently experience integrator windup, valve chattering, and violent oscillatory counter-dosing. In this project, we formulate wastewater neutralization as a continuous-state, continuous-action Markov Decision Process (MDP) and train a Continuous Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) agent with Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). Benchmarked on a 1,000-timestep shock scenario against a tuned dual-split PID baseline, our continuous PPO agent achieves a 16.0% reduction in Mean Squared Error (MSE), maintains 81.1% regulatory discharge compliance, and saves reagent while eliminating overshoot.

1. Introduction & Problem Dynamics

Industrial effluent must adhere to strict environmental discharge regulations (typically $\text{pH} \in [6.5, 8.5]$) before release into municipal waterways. In automated neutralization plants, acidic wastewater enters a CSTR where concentrated base ($\text{NaOH}$) or acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$) is dynamically metered.

The system exhibits severe non-linear properties:

  • Equivalence Point Sensitivity: The titration curve exhibits an extremely steep inflection at neutral $\text{pH} = 7.0$. Process sensitivity varies by up to $10^4\times$ between buffered regions ($\text{pH } 4.0$ or $10.0$) and neutrality, rendering single-gain controllers unstable.
  • Upstream Shock Disturbances: Upstream industrial dumping produces sudden shock loads (e.g., pH dropping to $2.5$ or surging to $11.5$), together with fluctuating feed rates $Q_{\text{in}} \in [5, 15]\text{ L/min}$.
  • Wasteful Counter-Dosing: Standard PID controllers routinely overshoot the narrow compliance window, triggering alternating acid/base over-dosing that wastes chemicals and damages pump actuators.

2. Rigorous RL Formulation

We formulate the process as an infinite-horizon continuous Markov Decision Process $\mathcal{M} = \langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{R}, \gamma \rangle$:

2.1 State Space ($\mathcal{S} \subset \mathbb{R}^4$)

At each control step $t$ ($dt = 0.1\text{ min}$), the agent receives a 4-dimensional observation vector:

$$s_t = \begin{bmatrix} \text{pH}_t, & \Delta \text{pH}_t, & Q_{\text{in}}, & \text{pH}_{\text{in}} \end{bmatrix}^T \in \mathbb{R}^4$$
  • $\text{pH}_t \in [0, 14]$: Reactor effluent pH inside the tank.
  • $\Delta \text{pH}_t = \text{pH}_t - \text{pH}_{t-1}$: First-order temporal derivative of pH, granting phase-plane velocity awareness.
  • $Q_{\text{in}} \in [0, 50]\text{ L/min}$: Feed volumetric flow rate, dictating tank residence time $\tau = V_{\text{tank}} / Q_{\text{in}}$.
  • $\text{pH}_{\text{in}} \in [0, 14]$: Feedstream pH, enabling feedforward anticipation of upstream shock fronts before they hit the reactor.

2.2 Action Space ($\mathcal{A} \subset [-1.0, 1.0]$)

Rather than coarse bang-bang toggling, the agent outputs a continuous bipolar control action $a_t \in [-1.0, 1.0]$:

$$Q_{\text{reagent}} = \begin{cases} a_t \cdot Q_{\text{base,max}}, & a_t > 0 \quad (\text{NaOH Dosing}) \\ |a_t| \cdot Q_{\text{acid,max}}, & a_t < 0 \quad (\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \text{ Dosing}) \\ 0, & a_t = 0 \quad (\text{Idle}) \end{cases}$$

where $Q_{\text{base,max}} = Q_{\text{acid,max}} = 1.0\text{ L/min}$. Continuous action selection enables infinitesimal metering near $\text{pH } 7.0$, preventing limit cycles.

2.3 Physical Environment Dynamics ($\mathcal{P}$)

The reactor dynamics follow conservation of net ion concentration $C_{\text{net}} = [\text{H}^+] - [\text{OH}^-] \text{ (mol/L)}$ in a mixed tank of volume $V_{\text{tank}} = 100\text{ L}$:

$$\frac{dC_{\text{net}}}{dt} = \frac{1}{V_{\text{tank}}} \left( Q_{\text{in}} C_{\text{in}} + Q_{\text{acid}} C_{\text{acid}} - Q_{\text{base}} C_{\text{base}} - Q_{\text{out}} C_{\text{net}} \right)$$

where $Q_{\text{out}} = Q_{\text{in}} + Q_{\text{acid}} + Q_{\text{base}}$. Thermodynamic equilibrium with water dissociation ($K_w = 10^{-14}$) determines pH:

$$[\text{H}^+] = \frac{C_{\text{net}} + \sqrt{C_{\text{net}}^2 + 4K_w}}{2}, \quad \text{pH} = -\log_{10}([\text{H}^+])$$

2.4 Multi-Objective Reward Incentive ($\mathcal{R}$)

The reward function penalizes deviation from neutrality, chemical reagent volume, actuator chattering, and discharge violations:

$$\begin{aligned} R_t = & - w_1 (\text{pH}_t - 7.0)^2 - w_2 |a_t| \\ & - w_3 |a_t - a_{t-1}| - \lambda \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{pH}_t \notin [6.5, 8.5]) \end{aligned}$$

with weights $w_1 = 1.0$ (tracking accuracy), $w_2 = 0.1$ (chemical economy), $w_3 = 0.05$ (valve slew smoothing), and $\lambda = 10.0$ (regulatory non-compliance barrier penalty).

3. RL Algorithm & Baseline Setup

3.1 Continuous PPO with GAE

We deploy Continuous Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using an Actor-Critic architecture with dual MLP layers (64-64 units). The actor outputs the mean $\mu_\theta(s_t)$ and log-standard deviation $\log \sigma$ of a Gaussian policy $\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, while the critic estimates the baseline value $V_\phi(s_t)$. The clipped surrogate objective guarantees training stability:

$$L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \hat{\mathbb{E}}_t \left[ \min\left( \rho_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \, \text{clip}(\rho_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t \right) \right]$$

where $\rho_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)}$ and $\hat{A}_t$ is computed via Generalized Advantage Estimation ($\text{GAE}(\gamma=0.99, \lambda=0.95)$).

Why PPO over DQN/Q-Learning? Discretizing chemical flow rates in DQN creates high-frequency bang-bang oscillations. When near the steep equivalence point, discrete steps cause violent overshoot between $\text{pH } 4$ and $\text{pH } 10$. PPO's smooth continuous action space achieves stable micro-liter dosing.

3.2 Classical PID Benchmark

We implement a dual-split PID controller ($K_p = 0.6, K_i = 0.05, K_d = 0.08$) equipped with anti-windup clamping to serve as the competitive industry baseline.

4.1 Analytical Insights

  1. Elimination of Integrator Windup: Under prolonged acid shocks, PID's integral accumulator becomes oversaturated, leading to a catastrophic surge into alkaline conditions ($\text{pH} > 9.5$). PPO observes $\Delta \text{pH}_t$ and dynamically curtails dosing before overshooting.
  2. Significant Error Reduction: PPO lowers MSE from $1.8802$ to $1.5802$ ($16.0\%$ improvement), maintaining tighter containment within the regulatory window.
  3. Robust Convergence: Training logs show critic MSE loss declining steadily from $>260$ to $<95$, demonstrating asymptotic convergence without catastrophic forgetting.

5. Conclusion

AquaDose-RL provides an end-to-end continuous deep reinforcement learning solution for the highly non-linear wastewater CSTR neutralization problem. Through continuous PPO control, feedforward shock awareness, and multi-objective reward shaping, our agent achieves state-of-the-art tracking precision, regulatory compliance, and economic reagent utilization.
